# Explainer video: the truncated dodecahedron is rigid

`video.mp4` (about 9 minutes 40 seconds, 1080p30, synthetic narration) explains the finding to a viewer who has just finished GCSE maths. It starts with a hinged square that wobbles and a triangle that does not. It then asks whether the truncated dodecahedron can wobble if its sticks keep their lengths and its faces stay flat. Counting cannot decide, because Euler's formula makes the count zero for every such solid. The video introduces first-order flexes as arrows that keep lengths unchanged at the first instant, shows a fake flex blocked at second order, and uses the solid's real numbers: four infinitesimal flexes, all 40 second-order numbers zero, and four cubic leftovers. A worked toy example (x squared minus y squared, and xy) gives the key idea, and the real certificate is then shown cancelling on a 1009-hour clock: 201, 182, 161 and finally one nonzero term, v0 to the ninth. The video explains why a clock that sees no wobble proves that the real solid has none, and ends by stating the scope: local rigidity, computer-assisted, a written proof that has not been checked in Lean or refereed externally. Every number comes from `../verification/` through `data_prep.py` (written to `data.json`). The real first-order flex and its second-order correction, used in the 3D and plot scenes, are computed numerically from the exact geometry. To rebuild, run `python data_prep.py` (needs numpy), then use `tools/video/make_audio.py <this folder>` (which only regenerates changed clips) and `tools/video/render.py <this folder> --quality h --contact` with the collection's video environment. The full 1080p render takes about 30 minutes.
