# Explainer video: tic-tac-toe where nobody wins

`video.mp4` (9 min 41 s, 1920x1080 at 30 fps, narrated) explains `HJ(p+1, 2; 2) = p+1` for every prime `p`. It opens with a lopsided tic-tac-toe, where red needs a whole line and blue needs only two neighbours on a line. It defines lines as star patterns and colours the 3 by 3 board, which is the `p = 2` case: the chessboard idea fails on the diagonal, and repairing the zero cell and the sum-one cells gives a draw. From there it covers what was known (Hales and Jewett; Conlon, Farnsworth and Robertson), the digit-sum colouring for every prime, why it works (equal hops along a line, and a clock argument that is the only place primality is used), the smallest new case `k = 8` with the exhaustive check counts from `../verification/`, what the Lean formalisation does and does not establish, and the open cases `k = 9` and `k = 10`. Every number on screen is reproduced by `check_numbers.py`. To rebuild from the repository root with the video environment's Python: `python tools/video/make_audio.py <this folder>` (only changed clips are regenerated), then `python tools/video/render.py <this folder> --quality h --contact`. `storyboard.md` has the scene plan and the first-time viewer review, and `subtitles.srt` the captions. The narration uses a synthetic voice.
