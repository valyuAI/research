# Explainer video: endless words that never repeat their shape

`video.mp4` (9 min 47 s, 1920x1080 at 30 fps, narrated) explains the finding that an endless binary word can avoid Cartesian-tree ("same shape") squares whose halves have 4 or more letters, and an endless ternary word can avoid those with halves of 3 or more. It builds up from zero: squares in words (banana), why two letters cannot avoid squares (tried live), Thue's three-letter result and Dejean's 7/4 word, the shape code drawn as columns with look-left hops, binary shape squares, and the 2026 table of Shibata, Mieno, Köppl and Inenaga whose question marks the result fills in. It then shows the construction (each letter of Dejean's word becomes `00101`, `00111` or `01100`), why a 7-letter window determines its middle letter (and a 5-letter window does not), how a long shape square becomes a near-exact square and then a forbidden repetition in Dejean's word (exponent at least 2 - 14/m, above 7/4 once m > 56), and the finite computer check (4,596 and 3,654 base words, no shape square). It closes with an honest status (computer-assisted, not checked in Lean, not peer reviewed, modest in scope) and open questions. Every example on screen is reproduced by `check_numbers.py`; the counts come from `../verification/`. To rebuild from the repository root with the video environment's Python: `python tools/video/make_audio.py <this folder>` (only changed clips are regenerated), then `python tools/video/render.py <this folder> --quality h --contact`. `storyboard.md` has the scene plan and the first-time viewer review, and `subtitles.srt` the captions. The narration uses a synthetic voice.
