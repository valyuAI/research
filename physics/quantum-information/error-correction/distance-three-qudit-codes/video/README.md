# Explainer video: every qudit code of distance three

`video.mp4` (9 minutes 53 seconds, 1080p30, synthetic narration) explains the finding to a viewer who has just finished GCSE maths. It starts with a yes/no answer sent over a noisy radio and the say-it-three-times repetition code, shows distance three as non-overlapping bubbles on the cube of 3-bit messages, and introduces parity questions, fingerprints and the counting limit through the [7,4] Hamming code. It then explains why quantum data can only be questioned, not read, introduces qutrits as three-setting dials with clock arithmetic, and follows one real qutrit (qutrit 14 of the stored [[28,23,3]]_3 certificate) whose nine error fingerprints form a sheet. Gluing each answer list to its double turns sheets into lines in a 121-point geometry, so a code is a set of skew lines; a bridge over a road, the seven-point plane and the ten-line tiling for four questions make that concrete. The key proof step is animated: 29 lines would leave 5 holes, every 40-point slice would need at least 2 (40 - 29 - 3x = 11, 8, 5, 2), and 121 x 2 = 242 exceeds 5 x 40 = 200. The video then builds the 28-line code from 27 slope lines plus one, and shows the quantum balance condition as a running total of ten clock numbers taken from the real construction (zero, then a leftover 1, then fixed by weights 2). It ends with the general theorem, the r = 5 values 28, 65 and 126, the twelve table entries, an honest status card (written proofs, 255 certificates and an independent checker, no Lean, no peer review, Thas 1971 cited) and the open distance-four case (16 known against a limit of 29 for qutrits with seven questions).

Every number is recomputed by `data_prep.py` from `../verification/` and written to `data.json`; the script also brute-forces the small geometry facts that are stated on screen. Simplifications: the 121-point field is drawn schematically (points of a line are drawn next to each other); the slice shown in the hole argument is a hypothetical one consistent with the count, since 29 lines do not exist; "shift" and "twist" stand for X- and Z-type errors; the Plucker relation is called a "signature" and is only described, not derived; the construction's weights 2, -1, 2 are shown as 2, 2, 2 because -1 equals 2 on a three-hour clock (the extra line's weight -2 likewise equals 1); impure codes get one sentence and one number (2 + 10 = 12); and the scope (F_q-linear stabilizer codes, all stabilizer codes for prime q) is shortened to "stabilizer codes".

Rebuild: `python3 data_prep.py`, then from the repository root, with the collection's video environment, `tools/video/make_audio.py <this folder>` (only regenerates changed clips) and `tools/video/render.py <this folder> --quality h --contact` (the full 1080p render took about 45 minutes).
